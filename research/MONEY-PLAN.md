# My money plan: R500 a month, 8 years

*Written 2026-09-29. Analysis to inform a decision, not regulated financial advice. Tax limits and
fund fees change, so confirm them with SARS and your provider before you act.*

**My situation:** 8-year horizon · no tax-free account yet · I can stomach a fall of less than 20% ·
R500 a month, automated.

## Do this, in order

- [ ] **Emergency fund first.** 3–6 months of expenses in a separate savings account. Do not invest this.
- [ ] **Clear expensive debt.** Anything costing more than about 8% a year comes before investing.
- [ ] **Open a TFSA.** R500 a month is R6,000 a year, well inside the annual limit (R36,000 at last
      check; the lifetime limit was R500,000). Growth is tax-free.
- [ ] **Pick one fund.** A South African multi-asset **low-equity** fund. That category is capped at
      40% shares by design, so it matches a loss limit near 20%. Compare total cost (TER) across
      2–3 funds and prefer under about 1% a year.
- [ ] **Set the debit order.** R500 a month, on the day after payday.
- [ ] **Raise it each year.** Increase the debit order by about 10% whenever your pay goes up.
- [ ] **Review once a year,** never once a week.

## Why this mix

Shares can fall 40–55% in a crash (2008 and 2020 did). Dividing a 20% loss limit by a 50% crash
gives about 40% in shares, with the rest in cash and bonds. This is a rule of thumb, not something my
backtest measured, and bonds and cash do not stay perfectly flat in a crisis.

Raising the amount by 10% each year lifts the 6% case to about R84,705 (R68,615 paid in).
A 20% fall on R61,000 is about R12,200. Early on, when the balance is small, a fall costs far less.

## My rules, decided before the crash

1. I will not sell if the fund falls 10–15%. Selling turns a temporary loss into a real one.
2. I will not touch this money for 8 years. Money needed within 3–5 years does not belong in shares.
3. I will not try to time the market. In my research, none of six timing rules beat holding.
4. If I would break rule 1, I hold less in shares. My real tolerance is what I do in a crash, not
   what I say now.

## What this plan does not cover

The research behind it tested the US index only. Nothing here uses South African fund returns,
and I have not compared specific funds. Verify current fees and performance before choosing one.
